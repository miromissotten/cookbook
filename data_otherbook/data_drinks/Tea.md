# The Essential Guide to Tea Categories

Tea categories are defined by their **processing styles** rather than the specific leaf material used. While all "true tea" comes from the *Camellia sinensis* plant, the method of preparation determines which of the six major types the tea becomes.

---

### ⚪ White Tea
*   **The Process:** These are dried tea leaves that naturally oxidize slightly during the drying process. Unlike green tea, white tea does not receive heavy heat early on to stop oxidation, resulting in off-green or even brown-colored finished leaves.
*   **Flavor Profile:** Very close to the "raw" state of the leaf; typically mild, floral, or vegetal.
*   **Origin:** Most varieties originate from China.

### 🟢 Green Tea
*   **The Process:** Leaves are heated (fired or steamed) very soon after picking to stop oxidation, leaving them practically unoxidized.
*   **Flavor Profile:** Vegetal, grassy, or sometimes marine (seaweed-like), especially in Japanese varieties. Profiles range from very mild to quite strong.
*   **Origin:** Major producers are China and Japan.

### 🟡 Yellow Tea
*   **The Process:** Similar to green tea, but with an added step called "smothering." The leaves are dampened and covered for a period, which allows for a very slight non-enzymatic oxidation.
*   **Flavor Profile:** Subdued, mellow, and smooth.
*   **Note:** This is a rare tea produced in only a few specific locations.

### 🔵 Oolong Tea
*   **The Process:** A partially oxidized tea. The leaves are lightly bruised (usually by shaking) to accelerate oxidation around the edges. Once the desired oxidation level is reached, heat is applied to stop the process. Most Oolongs are roasted 1–3 times at the end.
*   **Flavor Profile:** Highly diverse, ranging from light and floral (close to green tea) to dark and nutty (close to black tea).
*   **Origin:** Primarily produced in China and Taiwan.

### 🔴 Black Tea (Known as "Red Tea" in East Asia)
*   **The Process:** The leaves are heavily bruised to ensure thorough and rapid oxidation. After a few hours, the leaves are usually fully oxidized and then dried.
*   **Flavor Profile:** Strong, full-bodied, and earthy.
    *   **China:** Tends to be mellow, sweet, and earthy.
    *   **Assam (India):** Very strong, malty, and full-bodied.
    *   **Darjeeling (India):** Fruity and dry; "First Flush" (spring) is lighter/astringent, while "Second Flush" (summer) is mellower.
    *   **Ceylon (Sri Lanka):** Varies from fruity to malty.
*   **Origin:** China, India, and Sri Lanka.

### ⚫ Dark Tea (Known as "Black Tea" in East Asia)
*   **The Process:** This category relies on microbial ripening (fermentation). This can occur during active processing or as the tea ages in its finished form.
*   **Pu-erh Tea:** The most famous subcategory, produced almost exclusively in Yunnan, China. It comes in two types:
    *   **Sheng (Raw):** Unoxidized and aged naturally.
    *   **Shou (Ripe):** Accelerated fermentation via a piling process.
*   **Flavor Profile:** Very strong, complex, earthy, and medicinal. The flavor profile evolves significantly the longer it is aged.

### 🌿 Herbal Teas (Tisanes)
*   **The Process:** These are not "true" teas because they do not come from the *Camellia sinensis* plant.
*   **Definition:** Infusions made from herbs, roots, flowers, or fruits that are steeped and consumed in a manner similar to tea.
