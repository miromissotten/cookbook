### Description
- description: Bouillon is the soup which is used in [[_1.1.1. Ramen|ramen]] or as a base for [[_5.1.1. Miso|miso]] soup. The Japanese version is more savoury and less sweet than the French version. But just as always, experiment to your likings.

### Ingredients
- 300g onion (±2-3)
- 100g garlic bulbs (±1-2)
- 40g (±half a thumb) ginger
- 35g (±20cm) Kombu
- mushrooms options:
	- or: 17g dried Shiitake (or 170g fresh) + 17g dried Porcini (or 170g fresh) (replace with normal mushrooms or more shiitake if not available)
	- or: 400g mushrooms
- 400g (±0.5) green cabbage leaves (or white if you don't have green)
- 400g (±1) leek
- 75g (±6) spring onions

### Hardware
- airfryer (or oven or pot or skillet)
- instant pot (or large pot)
- blender/mixer
- sieve/strainer

### Instructions
- Place the whole onion, garlic bulbs, and ginger (all covered in a light layer of oil) in the airfryer for 00h15 at 180°C to roast them until they become light brown (the darker, the more umami, but the more risk for burnt taste which would ruin everything. Place your bets.)
- Transfer the air-fried things to the Instant Pot. Add kombu, shiitake, porcini, cabbage leaves, leek, and spring onions. Top up with water to the max fill line.
- Cook in the Instant Pot on high pressure for 00h30 (or soft boil in a regular large pot for 01h30). The short time is only applicable for the vegan version. If using meat, you will have to cook it for longer as the gelatinous part has to dissolve and be taken up in the stock.
- Carefully strain the bouillon, reserving the cooked garlic cloves. Watch out for the heat, don't burn yourself.
	- The garlic cloves are used later to make the stock into a [[_3.1.3.D. Bouillon_Tonkotsu_Vegan|vegan tonkotsu]] version. Continue with that recipe if you want a vegan tonkotsu. If you want clear stock, then you can ignore this step and don't have to reserve the cooked garlic cloves. they're a pain in the ass to fish them out of the vegetables, but my god it works wonders.

### Extra info
- Roasting the aromatics first adds a deep, sweet, and complex flavor base.
- If using dried shiitake or porcini, you can soak them in hot (non-boiling) water for 20 minutes beforehand to rehydrate them, adding both the mushrooms and the soaking liquid to the pot for extra flavor.
- The bouillon can be kept in the fridge for a week or frozen for months.
