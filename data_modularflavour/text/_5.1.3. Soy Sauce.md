- title: Soy sauce

### Text
Like flours, soy sauces are just as frustrating. Only looking at it on a shelf will never tell you what its taste is like. In general, if you have a bottle of Chinese light soy sauce and Chinese dark soy sauce, you're good to go.
#### Chinese soy sauces
Chinese light vs dark refers primarily to color and processing, not the amount of salt.

- Light soy sauce
	- thin, reddish-brown, relatively salty
	- simple seasoning soy sauce
	- adds salt + umami without big visual change
- Dark soy sauce
	- darker and thicker
	- adds color and richness than pure saltiness
	- lightly sweet/caramel-like with a big visual change when added
#### Japanese soy sauces
Again, light is only in colour, not by definition more salty

- Usukuchi
	- comparable to Chinese light soy sauce
- Koikuchi
	- comparable to Chinese dark soy sauce
- Tamari
	- good glutenfree alternative to general soy sauce
	- dark, rich, and relatively thick
	- strong umami and deeper flavor
- Saishikomi
	- Literally "twice brewed": Brewed using already-made soy sauce rather than just water
	- Very dark, rich, and complex

#### Indonesian soy sauce

- Kecap manis
	- Very sweet soy sauce
	- Thick and syrupy
