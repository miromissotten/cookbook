Ramen was my introduction to thinking about dishes as modular systems.

A bowl of ramen is built from 5 components which each have a distinct role and together create balance and a sense of completeness while not becoming boring halfway the meal.
All components can be endlessly experimented with. Once the structure is followed, it is surprisingly difficult to make a bowl of ramen that doesn't work.

Ramen is basically a soup with noodles in it. The broth provides the body and volume of the dish, while the tare gives it its main flavour. The noodles add structure and texture, the toppings introduce additional textures and irregular surprises, and the oils are magic sprinkles on top.

What makes it a prime example for modular cooking is that you can change any component while keeping the others constant, creating something that feels completely different while still following the same underlying architecture.

The saying goes:
> “A good ramen doesn't need toppings.”

If the broth, tare, and noodles are right, everything added on top becomes an opportunity rather than a necessity.
