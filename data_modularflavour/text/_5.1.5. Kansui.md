### Text
kansui is a catchall term for the alkaline salts you add to noodle dough to raise the pH of the water. As a starting point, you'd use roughly 1 to 2% of the weight of your flour. It's a small amount, but even a shift of a percentage point or two makes a measurable difference.

The shift in pH makes the gluten become more rigid and stronger under tension (noodles become 'harder' -> more brittle, but more rigid with less stretch). It creates the "bite".

Beyond texture, kansui does three other things worth knowing. 
- First, it makes the dough harder to roll and press — the alkaline environment is less forgiving, so you'll notice the difference when working it by hand or through a machine.
- Second, it turns the noodles slightly yellow. The alkalinity causes flavonoids in the flour to detach and change color — and the higher the pH, the brighter and more yellow the noodle becomes.
- Third, it gives ramen its signature taste: a faint eggy, almost sulphuric note that sounds strange on paper but feels completely right in the bowl. 

The two most common salts you'll encounter are sodium carbonate and potassium carbonate. Sodium pulls the noodle toward chewiness; potassium pulls it toward firmness. Most recipes blend both to find a balance.
