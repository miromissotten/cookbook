While the previous 3 architectures allowed for a good base to build a dish with, using the moto "less is more" in terms of ingredients, the layered architecture is a "more is more". The more layers you add, the better they get. Be it physical layers of the same dish or new layers of new ingredients. More is more.

In terms of intra-ingredients more-is-more, I can only think of 2 essential dishes which Flavia got me to see for the first time: the wonders of [[_1.4.1. Parmigiana|parmigiana]] and [[_1.4.2. Lasagna|lasagna]]. 

- [[_1.4.1. Parmigiana|Parmigiana]], I tasted for the first time in a tiny little restaurant in Anzio/Nettuno, and it was so deliciously fatty. It's a lasagna, but using eggplant instead of pasta layers for texture. 
- [[_1.4.2. Lasagna|Lasagna]] is the physical representation of love in a dish. If somebody makes you a lasagna, treasure them like you've never treasured before.

In terms of inter-ingredients more-is-more, I'm interested in 3 dishes:

- The first dish I got to know like this, is [[_1.4.3. Okonomiyaki|okonomiyaki]], and thus it's the first in the list. It's a japanese junk food dish. But I have to admit that even just thinking back about the experience of eating at a good okonomiyaki place, is magic on its own (thinking of LOPEZ okonomiyaki in Hiroshima).
- The second dish I got to know, was [[_1.4.4. Kumpir|kumpir]], which I got to know in Heimweh, Berlin. To this day, that place has not been beaten. Neither in terms of experience as in taste. At it's core, it's a potato with vegetables stuffed in it. The simpler something can be described, the better it is.
- The third dish I experimented with, was simple [[_1.4.5. Hamburgers|hamburgers]]. These are tricky to get right. If you eat meat, get the best steak you can possibly find, grind it up, and fry it. To this day, the best burger I ate was made by a friend, Lucas, and was buns, meat, a little salad against mushy buns, and a tiny dollop of sauce. It carries all weight. Once you go vegan, things get... interesting...
