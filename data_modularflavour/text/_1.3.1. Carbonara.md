### Ingredients
- 200 g spaghettoni (or whatever else you like as a shape, both long-form and short-form work)
- 2 whole eggs (you can play with the number of yolkes for more sweetness, but not essential to my taste)
- 80-100 g guanciale cut to small strips (or pancetta or other bacon-like things if not available)
	- Can be replaced with zucchini which is fried in soy sauce to mimic the salty taste.
- 50g hard cheese mix (I like 20%-40%-40%, but 0-100%-0 or 0-0-100% tastes close to perfect as well, just different)
	- pecorino romano for bitterness and sharpness
	- grana padano for creaminess and fattiness
	- parmesan for saltiness and mildness
- black pepper (fresh, coarsely ground)
- salt

### Hardware
- 1 pot 
	- larger makes it easier for boiling pasta
- 1 skillet 
	- I like a simple, quite big stainless steel one that can easily fit the entire dish for all people at the end
- 1 mixing bowl for bain marie
	- a thin metal one which almost but just not fits in the pot) is the easiest to work with
	- could also be done with a porcelain one (but heating and holding at the same time gets more tricky in step 2) or a different smaller pot (but avoiding solidification of some egg gets more tricky)
- whisk
- tongs (or chopstick if you're talented)

### Instructions
- step 1 of things in parallel (can be done slowly in sequence, but the dish feels made to be parallelized so why not)
	- In pot, bring salty water to a boil ("should taste like the sea", so enough salt) (I like to boil water with a kettle and pour it then on the pasta because I'm impatient)
	- In skillet fry guanciale over medium heat (no need for extra fat, any guanciale/pancette has enough fat on its own)
	- In mixing bowl, whisk eggs, cheese, and black pepper shortly (we'll whisk more later, just has to be combined for now)
- step 2 of things in parallel (again, most can be done in sequence, but parallel is more fun)
	- keep on watching the guanciale and shut it off when almost done (residual heat of the skillet will finish them anyway)
	- add a tsp of oil (cold) to the mixing bowl mixture. (For better taste, use some of the guanciale fat which has rendered in the skillet, but remember to let it cool in the spoon before adding to the mixture to avoid solidifying the eggs)
	- Add pasta to the pot
	- put the mixing bowl on top of the pot, and whisk continuously
		- make sure the eggs never set, but heat up enough to cook them through so they're not raw
		- move and scrape all sides so you never have any "surprise" solidified egg at the bottom of the sauce
		- if you see the eggs start to set, take the bowl out of the pot and mix more intensely to stop it before it spreads
		- the goal is to achieve a creamy sauce with cooked through eggs.
		- The endgoal of heating is when the sauce has the viscosity of oil
		- The tricky bit is that the sauce will remain more liquid as long as it's heated up and will solidify more when cooled down.
		- when desired consistency is reached, put the mixing bowl to the side
- when the pasta is done, transfer it to the skillet with the guanciale (which should be off for a while now) and mix it about for a bit
- you now have a few minutes of doing nothing but waiting to do...
	- clean up the pot in which the pasta was boiled as you don't need it anymore
	- ready the kitchen bowls and cutlery
	- share a kiss
- when you can place your hand on the pasta for >3 seconds without burning yourself, pour the sauce over the pasta and mix the pasta quickly to not have the sauce solidify
- Serve immediately (because rapid heat loss) with extra cheese (of choice) and black pepper on top
- If you have solidified eggs, something was still too hot. sorry. better luck next time.
