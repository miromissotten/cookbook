Each dish is built around a "first word". When you describe what you ate, you'll always start with a specific first word, which gives a visual image to the listener. That first word is what I want to describe in this chapter as the first word is (usually) the most filling ingredient of the dish: the carbohydrates source. 

You might hear that carbohydrates are bad for your health, but that's an oversimplification. Carbohydrates are molecules made up of one or more sugar (saccharide) units, making short or long chains. During digestion, many carbohydrates are broken down into simpler sugars, such as glucose, a short chain, at different rates depending on their structure and the food they're in. In candy, there are mainly short chains, easily digestible, which makes them raise your glucose quickly. In "brown food" (ex whole-grain rice), the chains are longer and take longer for your system to digest, thus not causing a spike in glucose. On the other hand, many processed flours contain long, but simplified chains, which also cause a glucose spike. 
Glucose spikes aren't bad, very often though, it gives your body a boost of energy, so much so that I can't use all and converts the overshot to fat for energy conversion for later times.
