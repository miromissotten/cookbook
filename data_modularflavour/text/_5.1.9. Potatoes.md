### Text

In potatoes, there's many variants. These are relatively easy to scale on one axis. More axes are possible, but potatoes aren't my favourite, so I don't notice the difference really...

Below you have a table of the typical types of potatoes and their classification on the one "starch-axis".

| Potato Type                      | Starch Level   | Notes                                               |
| -------------------------------- | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| **Russet / Idaho / Maris Piper** | High (starchy) | Fluffy interior, crispy edges when roasted or fried |
| **King Edward**                  | High (starchy) | Classic British roast potato                        |
| **Yukon Gold**                   | Medium         | Slightly creamy, good all-rounder                   |
| **Purple / Blue potatoes**       | Medium         | Adds color, slightly firm                           |
| **Red / New / Jersey Royal**     | Low (waxy)     | Holds shape, slightly moist                         |
| **Charlotte / Nicola**           | Low (waxy)     | Holds shape, delicate flavor                        |
| **Fingerling**                   | Low (waxy)     | Keeps shape, tender texture                         |

#### Roasting&Frying
- best from starchy to waxy (top to bottom)
- goal: crispiness

#### Salads&boiling: 
- best from waxy to starchy (bottom to top)
- goal: keep intact

#### Mash/Puree
- best from semi-starchy to starchy (middle to top)
- goal: texture + creaminess
- note: for French Puree (which is basically 50% butter&cream / 50% potatoes) use waxy potatoes to counter the fluff of the butter&cream. It's counterintuitive, but needs to compensate for the massive amount of butter.
