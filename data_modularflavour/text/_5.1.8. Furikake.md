### Text
Furikake (literally translated as "sprinkled over") is a dry Japanese seasoning combination. Its main use is on top of cooked rice, by adding both flavour and texture. 
#### A short history
Furikake was first used in Japan's Taisho period (±1920) as a supplement of calcium and other nutrients.
At first it was a blend of 
- ground dried fish bones
- roasted sesame seeds
- roasted poppy seeds
- roasted seaweeds
Its name was Gohan no Tomo (literally translated as "Friend for Rice").
During World War I and II, furikake got widely used by the Japanese army due to its combination of long shelf life, light weight, calcium and flavour. 
In 1959, the name "furikake" became official by an association. 
Then, with industrialisation, multiple variants were created and history becomes more tricky to track.
#### Today's simple furikake
*   40% roasted white sesame seeds
*   30% flaked nori seaweed
*   20% katsuobushi
*   10% seasoning (salt+sugar)
#### Today's variations
The basic version has the most widespread use. Variations on this basic version got to exist to better coexist with regional specialties, or simply because other ingredients were available in the kitchen.

| Furikake type               | Description                                                                          |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Katsuo Furi             | basic furikake + more soy-glazed bonito flakes and nori                              |
| Noritama                | basic furikake + sweetened, freeze-dried egg granules                                |
| Shiso Furikake (Yukari) | Dried, salted, and crushed red-purple perilla leaves; more herbal and floral profile |
| Tarako/Mentaiko         | Sesame + nori + freeze-dried salted cod roe / spicy pollock roe                      |
| Wasabi Furikake         | basic furikake + dried wasabi                                                        |
| Goma Shio               | Roasted black sesame seeds + coarse crystal salt                                     |
