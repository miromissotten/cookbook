### Text
Flours can be classified by what they are made from, protein/gluten content, and degree of refinement/mineral content. Depending on your country of origin, you'll be used to different namings. Below the most classical classifications I've encountered.

After reading this, you'll probably be as frustrated as I am. It's a multidimensional scale and I hate it.

#### French T-Type Classification
The French T-number (Type) indicates approximately how much mineral content (~="whole-grain-ness") remains in the flour after burning. A higher number means more of the grain has been retained. (T55 ~= 0.55% minerals).
It however, does not indicate the protein nor gluten content. These are indicated separately and can vary wildly within the same T-number flours.

| Type | Character                   | Typical Uses                               |
| ---- | --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| T45  | Very white (refined & fine) | Cakes, pastries, brioche, viennoiseries    |
| T55  | \|                          | Baguettes, bread, pizza, general baking    |
| T65  | \|                          | Artisan bread, baguettes, sourdough        |
| T80  | \|                          | Rustic bread, sourdough, country loaves    |
| T110 | \|                          | Whole-grain bread, rustic loaves           |
| T150 | Whole grain                 | Dense whole-grain bread, high-fiber baking |

#### Italian Flour Types
Italian wheat flour is commonly classified as 00, 0, 1, 2, based primarily on how refined the flour is.

| Italian Type  | Character                    | Typical Uses                           |
| ------------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| 00        | Very refined, white          | Pasta, pizza, cakes, fresh pasta       |
| 0         | Refined, slightly less fine  | Bread, pizza                           |
| 1         | More of the grain retained   | Bread, pizza, rustic baking            |
| 2         | High-extraction / semi-whole | Rustic bread, whole-grain-style baking |
| Integrale | Whole wheat                  | Wholemeal bread and baking             |

Italian 00 does not automatically mean low protein. A 00 flour can be specifically formulated for strong pizza dough and have relatively high protein.

#### Wheat Flour by Gluten Content
Protein content is directly related to how a flour affects the dough.

| Flour             | Approx. Protein | Characteristics                                        | Typical Uses                  |
| ----------------- | --------------: | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Pastry flour      |             ~9% | Very soft, low gluten                                  | Pastry, cookies, pies         |
| All-purpose flour |            ~11% | \|                                                     | Anything                      |
| Bread flour       |            ~13% | \|                                                     | Bread, pizza                  |
| High-gluten flour |           ~14%+ | Very strong and elastic due to high gluten development | Specialty breads, chewy pizza |
