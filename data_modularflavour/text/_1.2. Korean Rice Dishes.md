Whereas ramen is liquid with noodles, the Korean food I want to talk about is solid with rice. You could consider it the same, but it deserved of having a separate chapter just like Ramen deserved a dedicated chapter. 
The Korean food I am regarding as an architecture are not stews (jjigae) or Korean BBQ, but bibimbap and kimbap. Simplified, [[_1.2.1. Kimbap&Sushi|kimbap]] is the cooked and/or baked version of sushi. I've never been a big fan of sushi because of the raw ingredients, and the discovery of kimbap solved it for me. [[_1.2.2. Bibimbap|Bibimbap]] can be considered as a decomposed makisu (not necessarily with the nori, but that's not the point right now...). Bibimbap is the extravagant luxury version of kimbap because you can add so many more things to it in separate side-dishes. So far, the best version I hope to one day compete with is, the one of Bibijae, Busan.

What I like about it, is that once again, the base is easy, it is modular, and it's hard to make it badly. If it's made badly, you can add more spicy sauces and it'll taste good anyhow.
