A band with 3 bass guitarists would be muddy as they occupy the same frequency space. Though a guitar, bass and drums for rock, Ever wonder why a rock band with a harmonica can work though it's not "standard"? That's because it occupies the same space as a guitar. a slapping bass and sparkly guitar for funk, a string orchestra with a horn section for movie themes,... You can do whatever you want and combine any instrument you want. If you take into account the frequency space of the instruments, a basic high-level script, it's bound to work out. 
This concept also applies within the food world. If the taste of each item is taken into account, like frequency space in a band, then within each system you can build whatever you feel like. When adding spice to spice, you'll get superspice, and when adding spice to sweet you get balance. Taking into account the value of each item, it's quasi impossible to mess up.

This first chapter are the leading dishes which made me interested in cooking and shaped my daily life. I like to call them architectural recipes, as they've been more design-concepts than actual rules to adhere to. Each one is modular, they can be adapted as much as you like to what you're into at that moment. It's the only chapter containing recipes of dishes in the entire book. The other chapters contain recipes for components, which can be used to make the recipes of the dishes.

As a human, I like contrast. In this book, I also like contrast, with the main idea being "less is more" and "more is more".
The first three sections of architectural recipes are "less is more", where the balance takes precedence over quantity. These are my day to day cooking upgrades I get into. Whenever I need to make anything happen and it needs to sparkle more than "carbs & vegetables in a pot", I resort to finding a technique from one of these 3. We'll see [[_1.1.
