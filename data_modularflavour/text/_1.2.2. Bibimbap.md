### Description
- description: Bibimbap literally means "mixed rice". If you replace the rice with noodles, you get Bibimguksu, "mixed noodles". It's basically a bowl with rice. Can be served hot and cold. My favourite weekly dish as it changes each time.

### Ingredients
- 176g cooked short-grain white rice (or 86-90g dry rice)
	- (steaming instead of cooking gives it an extra lightness)
	- (if you use 90% white rice and 10% black/venus rice, the rice is purple, which is really pretty)
- ...the rest of the toppings is up to you, I suggest picking any 4 of the list...
	- beef (bulgogi-style sliced or ground)
	- spinach
		- could be fresh or cooked, whichever you prefer
		- if cooked, frozen spinach tastes just as good as fresh once heated and takes a lot less storage space
	- bean sprouts (mung bean or soybean), shortly fried in a pan
	- [[_4.1.2. Miso Zucchini|miso zucchini]]
	- [[_4.1.3. Gochujang Mushrooms|gochujang mushrooms]]
	- [[_4.1.5. Sweet Potato|sweet potato]]
	- [[_4.1.8. Kimchi|kimchi]]
	- [[_4.1.4. Sesame Carrots|sesame carrots]]
	- eggs (can be boiled, an omelet, raw (if you're serving in a hot dolsot), ...)
	- gochujang (Korean chili paste) or ssamyang (Korean soy bean paste), to taste (optionally mixed with a bit of tahini)
	- toasted sesame oil
	- toasted sesame seeds
	- [[_4.1.10. Quick-Pickled Red Onions|quick-pickled red onions]]
	- [[_4.1.12. Quick-Pickled Cucumbers|quick-pickled cucumbers]]
	- Or anything else you can think of or have in the fridge

### Hardware
- Pan (or multiple pans or oven to speed up cooking)
- Pot (to cook rice) 
- Bowls for serving (dolsot/stone bowl optional but nice impressive touch)
- Knife and cutting board

### Instructions
- If dolsot available, lightly oil the inside and put it in a hot oven or on a gas stove to heat up. On a gas stove it is more difficult to gauge the temperature and duration, so up to you to figure it out. Just take into account that a dolsot is a granite pot, and granite pots can withstand very high temperatures, but only when slowly introduced to them. Once the oil sizzles, you can take it out/off.
- Cook rice and keep warm
- Prepare toppings
- if no dolsot available: fry eggs sunny-side-up
- Assemble: 
	- Add hot rice to the bowl. Arrange the prepared vegetables and beef on top in neat sections. It's not essential, but it's pretty so do it.
	- if using a dolsot, place the raw egg on top for the guest to mix
	- if not using a dolsot, place the fried egg in the center.
- Serve with sesame seeds and gochujang/sammjang and a bit of soy sauce (or replace it entirely with another [[_3.4.1. Deep & Savoury|deep and bold sauce]] you like. There's no rules.)
- If using a dolsot, wait a few minutes before serving so the base of the nurungji is already formed and serve it with barley tea (or any other) on the side. After the meal, the guest can poor the barley tea into the bowl to dissolve the formed nurungji. This makes it a little soup for the end of the meal, called sungnyung.
### Extra info
- The rice can be precooked a day in advance and kept in the fridge as well as all the other toppings you want to add. This transforms the dish from a dish with lots of work, to a dish with very little remaining work when the guests have arrived.
- Bibimbap has a very rich [[_5.1.6. History_of_bibimbap|history]], though nobody has any certainty on the real origins.
- Adjust the amount of gochujang to your preferred spice level. Slightly enhanced version would be mixing gochujang with sesame oil, a little sugar, and some water. But I suggest to go wild in experimentation.
