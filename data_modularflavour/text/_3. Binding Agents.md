Binding agents are the items which transform the list of ingredients to a cohesive dish. There are multiple ways to do this. In this section, 4 will be discussed.

The first part is about [[_3.1. Soups|soup]]. Now, soup is a broad term as it contains both liquids with blended vegetables, as liquids with suspended vegetables/meat, as liquids where all solids were extracted in advance.

The second part are the lightest ways to bind simple ingredients together, with [[_3.2. Aromatic oils|oils]]. Oils get their power in lightness, and still bringing an aroma which can bind the flavours of all components.

The third part are classical [[_3.3. Italian Emulsions|italian emulsion sauces]]. These are the ones I use most, and never fail to bind everything together. 

The fourth and final part is an elaboration on the third part, with more [[_3.4. Distinct Sauces|sauces]]. These are flavour-based, while part three was texture-based. This is the most extensive list of the four parts, though least technical and thus less likely to mess up.

The further you go in this chapter, the more the components are traditionally used as a binding agent and thus the most secure of an option they are. The less traditional, the more they can also be used as a sprinkle of magic dust on an already complete dish. Ie the soups aren't very "sprinkly", but the aromatic oils very much are. The others are dependent on your creativity. They're a trick up your sleeve you can always use if the dish misses a little something and you want to add a big something.
