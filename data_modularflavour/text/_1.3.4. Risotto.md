### Ingredients
- 1l bouillon
- 300g arborio rice
- 300g (±2) onions
- 3g (1 clove) garlic
- 1 courgette (±300 g) / 250 g mushrooms / 3 radishes / … feel free to improvise
- ~50g hard cheese
	- to taste; Parmigiano for bitterness, Grana padano for fattiness. I like 50%/50%
- Butter 25-30 g
- Wine 150 ml
- Thyme
	- or whichever hard herb you prefer; I just really like thyme

### Hardware
- big pan
- pot 1l
- or forget everything else and just get a pressure cooker
### Instructions
There's a simple weekday version which doesn't need a lot of time, but also a version which looks very impressive and involved (though remains very simple in actions to do)
#### lazy version:
1. Put all ingredients (except cheese) together in the pressure cooker.
2. Cook 5 min on High Pressure + quick release → remove pot contents.
3. Add cheese.
4. Mix together until soft and more “sauce-like.”

#### impress-your-date version:
1. put the onion in a pan and fry with olive oil until slightly glazy look
	- The slower you fry the onion (lower heat), the sweeter it gets, but the more time it takes. Up to you to decide if time or taste is most essential during this date.
2. start to boil a kettle
3. add to the pan minced/chopped garlic and the courgette (or other vegetable(s))
4. when the courgette (or vegetable) starts to get light brown spots, add rice dry to the pan and add butter (or olive oil, but butter tastes better) to the rice.
5. create the bouillon using a stock cube or bouillon you prepared beforehand.
6. fry the rice (and vegetables) for a bit longer until the rice starts to have the tiniest hint of brown.
7. at that moment, add a ladle of stock (±150ml) to the pan. It will feel like you just unleashed hell for 30 seconds, but it should quickly reside. Keep on stirring.
8. each time when you can see the pan's bottom when scraping with a spoon, you can add more stock, little by little until after about 30 minutes, you'll probably have added all the liquid and the rice should be good.
9. with the last (small) ladle of liquid, add the rasped cheese and continue to stir until the consistency of the risotto resembles muesli in yoghurt.
10. If by now you haven't lost your date due to all the waiting in the kitchen for you, marry her.
11. best served when cooled a little, but hunger is the best sauce so you decide.
