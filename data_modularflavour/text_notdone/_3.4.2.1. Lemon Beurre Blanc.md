### Ingredients
- 120 ml dry white wine
- 60 ml white wine vinegar
- 15 g finely chopped shallot
- 200 g unsalted butter, cold
- Kosher or sea salt, to taste

### Hardware
- Small saucepan
- Whisk
- Optional: Fine-mesh sieve

### Instructions
- Cut butter to pieces
- In a small saucepan, combine the white wine, white wine vinegar, and chopped shallots.
- Bring the mixture to a boil over medium-high heat, then reduce the heat and let it simmer for about 10 minutes, or until the liquid has reduced to approximately 2 tablespoons.
- Lower the heat to the lowest setting and begin adding the cold butter cubes one or two at a time, whisking continuously.
- Continue adding the butter as it melts and emulsifies into the sauce.
- Once you have a few butter cubes left, remove the saucepan from the heat and continue whisking until all the butter is incorporated.
- Season generously with salt to taste.
- For a smoother sauce, you can strain the shallots out using a fine-mesh sieve, but they can also be left in for more flavor.
- Serve immediately.

### Extra info
- Beurre Blanc, which translates to "white butter," is a classic French sauce that originated in the Loire Valley region of France. [3, 4] It was reportedly created by accident in the early 20th century by chef Clémence Lefeuvre. [6]
- It is important to use cold butter and to add it to the reduction off the heat or on very low heat to prevent the sauce from separating. [3]
- If the sauce does separate or "break," you can sometimes fix it by taking it off the heat and whisking in a tablespoon of cold water.
- This versatile sauce pairs well with fish, seafood, poultry, and vegetables. [2] A variation called "beurre rouge" can be made by substituting red wine and red wine vinegar. [5]
