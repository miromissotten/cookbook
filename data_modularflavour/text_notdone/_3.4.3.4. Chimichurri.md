### Ingredients
- 1 cup packed fresh parsley, finely chopped
- 3-4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp fresh oregano leaves, finely chopped (or 2 tsp dried)
- 1/2 cup extra virgin olive oil
- 3 tbsp red wine vinegar
- 1 tsp red pepper flakes (or to taste)
- 1 tsp coarse salt
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

### Hardware
- Sharp knife and cutting board
- Mixing bowl
- Whisk or fork
- Optional: Food processor

### Instructions
- **Manual Method (Recommended for best texture):**
    - Finely chop the parsley, fresh oregano (if using), and mince the garlic. Place them in the mixing bowl.
    - Add the dried oregano (if using), salt, black pepper, and red pepper flakes.
    - Pour in the red wine vinegar and stir to combine.
    - Slowly whisk in the olive oil until the sauce is well emulsified.
- **Food Processor Method:**
    - Add parsley, garlic, and oregano to the food processor. Pulse a few times until finely chopped, but not a paste. [3]
    - Transfer the herb mixture to a bowl.
    - Stir in the salt, pepper, red pepper flakes, red wine vinegar, and finally whisk in the olive oil. [7]
- Let the chimichurri sit for at least 10-20 minutes before serving to allow the flavors to meld. For best results, let it rest for a couple of hours. [5]

### Extra info
- Chimichurri is an uncooked sauce originating from Argentina and Uruguay, traditionally served with grilled meats (asado). [13, 15]
- The key to a good chimichurri is to chop the ingredients by hand; pureeing them in a food processor can result in a bitter taste and a texture that is too smooth, more like a pesto. [7, 14]
- The flavor improves significantly after resting. You can make it a day ahead and store it in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to two weeks. [2] Bring to room temperature before serving.
- **Variations:**
    - Some recipes include fresh cilantro, though this is not in the most traditional versions. [3, 11]
    - For a different acidic kick, you can substitute or add some fresh lemon juice. [13]
    - Add finely chopped shallot or red onion for another layer of flavor. [3]
