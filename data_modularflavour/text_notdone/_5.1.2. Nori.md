### Text
#### What is nori?

**Nori (海苔)** is a type of edible seaweed, most commonly made from cultivated **Pyropia** seaweed. It is dried into thin sheets and is especially associated with Japanese cuisine.

Its flavor is typically **salty, savory, slightly sweet, and strongly umami**, with a crisp texture when freshly toasted.

#### Main types of nori

**1. Yaki-nori (焼き海苔)**
- The most common type of nori
- Already **roasted/toasted**
- Dark green to nearly black
- Crisp and slightly smoky
- Used for sushi rolls, onigiri, rice dishes, and as a topping

**Best for:** everyday cooking and sushi

**2. Nori (dried/raw nori)**
- Dried but not necessarily roasted
- Softer and more flexible than yaki-nori
- Usually needs to be toasted before eating
- Less common in Western supermarkets

**Best for:** toasting yourself or traditional preparations

**3. Ajitsuke-nori (味付け海苔)**
- **Seasoned nori**
- Usually brushed or coated with soy sauce, salt, sugar, and sometimes oil
- Thin, crisp, and strongly flavored
- Commonly eaten directly as a snack or with rice

**Best for:** eating with plain rice or as a snack

#### Nori grades

Nori is also sold in different quality grades. The terminology isn't completely standardized, but you'll commonly encounter distinctions based on **quality, harvest, and intended use**.

**Premium / high-grade nori**
- Dark, glossy appearance
- Fine, delicate texture
- More tender and less papery
- Stronger, cleaner umami
- Often used for high-quality sushi

**Standard nori**
- More uniform and affordable
- Slightly coarser texture
- Good for everyday sushi, onigiri, and cooking

**Lower-grade nori**
- May be lighter green or less uniform
- More brittle or coarse
- Flavor can be less complex
- Often perfectly suitable for cooking or crushing into toppings

#### Color differences

**Deep green / almost black**
- Usually associated with better-quality, fresher nori
- Often has a more pronounced umami flavor
- Glossy appearance is generally desirable

**Green / olive green**
- Can indicate a different grade, harvest, or processing
- Not automatically "bad"
- Often perfectly suitable for everyday use

**Brownish or yellowish**
- Can indicate age, oxidation, or lower quality
- Flavor and crispness may be reduced

Color alone isn't a reliable way to judge quality because **roasting, storage, harvest, and processing all affect appearance**.

#### Nori for different uses

| Type | Texture | Flavor | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yaki-nori | Crisp | Clean, savory | Sushi, onigiri, cooking |
| Unroasted nori | Soft/flexible | More vegetal | Toasting yourself |
| Ajitsuke-nori | Very crisp | Salty, sweet, savory | Snacking, rice |
| Premium nori | Fine, delicate | Rich umami | High-quality sushi |
| Lower-grade nori | Coarser | Milder | Toppings, furikake, cooking |

#### Nori vs other seaweeds

**Nori**
- Thin sheets
- Mild-to-strong umami
- Used as a wrapping or garnish
- Sushi, onigiri, snacks

**Kombu**
- Thick, leathery dried kelp
- Extremely rich in glutamates
- Primarily used to make **dashi**
- Usually not eaten as a sheet like nori

**Wakame**
- Thin, leafy seaweed
- Soft and slightly chewy after rehydration
- Common in miso soup and salads

**Hijiki**
- Small, dark, branching strands
- Earthy and savory
- Traditionally used in Japanese side dishes

#### What to look for when buying nori

For general-purpose use, look for:

- **Yaki-nori** if you want ready-to-use sheets
- A **dark green/black, relatively glossy** appearance
- Sheets that are **crisp rather than limp**
- Packaging that protects it from moisture
- A recent production/expiration date

For sushi, **higher-grade nori** is worth considering because texture and flavor are much more noticeable when the sheet is eaten whole.

For cooking, toppings, or crushing into flakes, you generally don't need premium nori.

#### How to store nori

Nori absorbs moisture very easily.

- Keep it **airtight**
- Store away from humidity
- Don't leave the package open
- If it becomes soft, briefly toast it over low heat to restore some crispness
- Once opened, use it relatively quickly for the best texture

**Bottom line:** for most people, **yaki-nori is the one to buy**. Choose premium nori when you're eating the sheet prominently (especially sushi or onigiri), and standard nori for everyday cooking and toppings.
